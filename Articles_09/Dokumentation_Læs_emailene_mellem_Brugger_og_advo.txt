Title: Dokumentation_Læs_emailene_mellem_Brugger_og_advo
Date: 2012-08-16
Article Type: Kultur
Author: Andreas Lindqvist

Otte emails mellem Mads Brügger og advokaten Varney Sherman viser, at alt ikke er som det fremlægges i 'Ambassadøren'.
Politiken.dk har siden bedt Brügger bekræfte autenciteten af emailene, men uden at kunne få et præcist svar.
»Nu har jeg kigget på mailene, og noget af det kan jeg genkende, og andet ser redigeret ud, så rent formelt kan jeg ikke bekræfte at de er ægte«, skriver Mads Brügger.
Vi præsenterer mailene på originalsproget med den første først.
Brügger optrådte under arbejdet med filmen under sit fødenavn Cortzen.

Gentlemen:
I have no news for you and I am sorry about that. Dr. Toga Gaywea McIntosh has not yet been confirmed by the Liberian Senate; he is still Acting Minister of Foreign Affairs.
I recall advising you that appointment of an Honrary Consul to a country is not an action that can be taken by an Acting Minister. Dr. McIntosh can only perform functions which are absolutely necessary to be performed until after he is confirmed by the Liberian Senate and receives his formal commission as Minister of Foreign Affairs.
This is the process and procedure in Liberia and there is nothing that I can do under the circumstances to expedite Mr. Cortzen's application to be Liberia's Honorary Consul to the Central African Republic.
Best regards.

Dear Gentlemen:
On the request of my friend, Mr. Wilfred Cooke, I agreed to assist Mr. Mads Johan Cortzen get an appointment as Liberia's Honorary Consul to Denmark. When he changed his mind and decided that he would prefer to be Liberia's Honorary Consul to the Central African Republic, I agreed to continue my assistance.
During the course of this I have personally taken both of you to the Ministry of Foreign Affairs to meet two Deputy Ministers and the Minister Akerele. At that time we were advised that since Liberia did not have any direct relationship with the CAR and considering that none of its citizens were resident in the CAR, an Honorary Consularship was not justifiable. Nevertheless I continue my assistance.
When we thought that things were on course, Foreign Minister Akerele was relieved of the post and Dr. McIntosh was appointed as successor. Since then I have made efforts to get the application revived only to discover that none of the papers could be found.
I advised you and assisted in drafting a new letter to revive the application, which Mr. Cortzen sent and which was delivered to the Ministry of Foreign Affairs for the attention of Dr. McIntosh. This letter was delivered to Deputy Minister Demmah, who had shown significant interest in assisting us get the appointment for Mr. Cortzen.
I believe that I have done my best to get the appointment for Mr. Cortzen, but it would appear that Mr. Cortzen is too impatient for the bureaucracy that surrounds this process.
Accordingly, hereby terminate our relationship and wish to advise that I shall not give any additional assistance to Mr. Cortzen as of this moment. Please also be advised that funds left with me to be used for this process continue to be in my possession.
You will recall that I suggested that you take back the funds and let's do a "success fee" arrangement but you insisted that I keep the funds here in Liberia to facilitate the process and cover expenses if need be. Those funds are available at your convenience for disposition as you wish.
Meanwhile, please be advised that I leave Liberia on March 16 for Europe and the United States and will not be returning to Liberia before April 16. It might therefore be advisable that before March 16 you take delivery of the funds or instruct on what disposition you would want. Otherwise you will get the funds upon my return to Liberia in April.
Best regards.
