Misinformation in the Time of Global Crises
Join a free after-work talk with climate journalists from around the world, where we focus on misinformation and its ability to weaken democracy’s capacity to act in the midst of a climate crisis.
The climate crisis is urgent. So is the problem of misinformation, which undermines our ability to act collectively. When facts are diluted and trust in media and knowledge is weakened, democracy and its ability to act are also weakened.
Therefore, Politiken and Danida invite you to an after-work talk and debate, where we illuminate the global challenges of misinformation. Experience climate journalists
Manish Mishra
from INDIA,
Dianah Chiyangwa
from SOUTH AFRICA,
Gisele Lobato
from BRAZIL, and
Nduta Mukami
from KENYA, who will each talk about local challenges with misinformation and provide concrete examples of the many faces of manipulation. This includes the public discourse on extreme weather, agriculture, industry, and energy. Politiken’s climate journalist
Adam Hannestad
will be asking the questions.
Learn more about how we should approach the issues in a panel discussion with climate ambassador
Ole Thonke
, biologist and environmental journalist
Alexander Holm
, editor-in-chief at TjekDet.dk
Steen Jensen
, and
Anne Højer Simonsen
, deputy director at DI, subsequently will discuss what misinformation is doing to the climate debate right now and how we can strengthen resilience against misinformation. And then Adam Hannestad asks: How can the media communicate climate science so that it reaches larger parts of the population? And what responsibility does a country like Denmark have in combating misinformation?
Note: The talk and the subsequent debate will be in English.
The event is created in collaboration with Danida Fellowship Centre.
